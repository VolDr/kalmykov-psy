# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Burnt-out IT and knowledge-work professionals, primarily in Russia and Russian-speaking contexts. Educated, analytical, skeptical of "soft" approaches. They've already tried productivity systems, vacations, and self-help; nothing has worked. They land here mid-crisis or in chronic low-grade exhaustion. They need to be met as peers, not patients. Secondary users: other professionals and doctors who refer clients.

## Product Purpose

Online psychological consultations specializing in burnout, anxiety, depression, perfectionism, and procrastination. The therapist (Andrey Kalmykov) uses ACT and CBT — evidence-based, structured, goal-oriented. The site must convert skeptical professionals into paying clients by demonstrating competence and specificity. Success means a booked first session. The admin panel and assessment tools serve the ongoing client relationship, not acquisition.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, rigorous, human.

Not warm in the way therapy brands usually are. Not clinical in the way medical brands usually are. The voice is that of a peer who happens to know what's actually happening and what to do about it.

## Anti-references

- **Generic therapy sites**: Stock photos of people meditating in natural light, soft gradients, pastel blues and greens, lotus flowers, "your journey starts here" copy. This is the category default. Avoid entirely.
- **Corporate wellness apps**: Headspace/Calm aesthetic — smooth blobs, soothing purples, mindfulness-as-product polish. Looks like a subscription rather than a relationship.
- **Startup landing pages**: Hero metrics, gradient CTAs, social proof carousels, SaaS-cream backgrounds, the "one tool to solve your life" promise.
- **Old-school clinical sites**: Dark navy, serif body text, couch-in-office photos, formal language, sterile distance. Signals institutional authority rather than a specific human.

No strong positive reference. The design should feel singular — emerging from the content and the audience, not from referencing another site.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust, don't perform it.** The audience is analytical and skeptical. No transformational promises, no before/after language. Show the method, the credentials, the specific approach. Specificity is more persuasive than warmth.

2. **The therapist is the brand.** This is not a platform or a category product. It is one specific person with a specific history, approach, and perspective. Design choices should reinforce that singularity — personality and specificity over category-generic patterns.

3. **Evidence over aesthetics.** Every design choice should serve a cognitive purpose. Restraint in decoration signals rigor in method. Ornamentation competes with credibility.

4. **Clarity as care.** The audience often arrives in distress — cognitively loaded, sleep-deprived, looking for reasons to leave. Keep hierarchy clear, flows predictable, language direct. Reduce cognitive friction at every step.

5. **Content leads, design follows.** The copy is doing the persuasion work. Typography, spacing, and structure amplify without competing. Let the text breathe.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Explicit attention to Cyrillic legibility: generous line-height for Russian text, care around hyphenation and line breaks with long compound words. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout. The audience may be in acute psychological distress — error messages and form feedback stay calm and non-judgmental in tone.
